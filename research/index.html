Research

Multi-organ proteomics across the ulcerative colitis continuum

Mapping systemic responses across healthy, diseased, and relapsed states.

As part of a major National Natural Science Foundation of China project, we profile multiple organs across the healthy–disease–relapse continuum of ulcerative colitis. The goal is to identify extraintestinal protein-level changes that accompany disease transition. The current work is based on animal models and clinical samples; causal roles of candidate proteins remain to be tested.

Serum proteomics and machine learning for biomarker discovery

Low-abundance enrichment, feature selection, and PRM validation.

We enrich low-abundance serum proteins, acquire proteomic profiles, and use machine learning to prioritize candidate biomarkers for HFpEF and Helicobacter pylori infection. Candidates are then validated by PRM targeted mass spectrometry. The main boundary is that candidate panels still need independent, adequately powered cohorts before clinical use.

Ferroptosis mechanisms of hepatoprotective traditional medicines

APAP, LPS/D-GalN, and CCL4 models dissected by multi-omics.

We build acetaminophen-, LPS/D-GalN-, and CCL4-induced liver injury models and combine proteomics, transcriptomics, and molecular biology to dissect ferroptosis and PPARγ/GPX4 signaling. This work supports a mechanistic axis for Yindan Pinggan capsules, but the evidence is preclinical and does not establish clinical efficacy.

Spatial omics and mass spectrometry imaging

Resolving region-specific lipids and proteins in tissue.

We use MALDI-MSI and spatial proteomics to resolve region-specific lipid and protein distributions in tissue, including ventricular remodeling after heart failure and TCM intervention. The approach is primarily exploratory: spatial patterns generate hypotheses, while cell-type assignment and causality require orthogonal validation.

Early prediction of prolonged ICU stay

2026 · Independent project · Python, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP, Jupyter, LaTeX

Problem. Prolonged ICU stay affects bed turnover, staffing, and discharge planning. Can routinely collected data from the first 24 h after ICU admission identify patients likely to stay for 72 h or longer?

Approach. I used the openly available MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo v2.2 (100 patients, 128 first ICU stays) and extracted 17 demographic, vital-sign, laboratory, and early-intervention variables. After a stratified 70/30 split, I compared L2-regularized logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost with five-fold cross-validated AUROC, then evaluated calibration, decision curves, and SHAP values on the held-out test set.

Result. Random forest achieved the highest cross-validated AUROC (0.778) and a test AUROC of 0.739 (95% CI 0.559–0.883), with PR-AUC 0.698 and Brier score 0.204. Logistic regression had a comparable test AUROC (0.750) and better calibration (Brier 0.183). Minimum GCS, maximum temperature, maximum lactate, mechanical ventilation, and maximum heart rate were the most influential predictors.

Boundary. The demo contains only 100 patients and 48 events, comes from a single center, and has no external validation. This is a reproducible methodological demonstration, not a clinical conclusion.

中文摘要。 本项目基于 MIMIC-IV Demo v2.2，纳入 128 个成人首次 ICU 住院，使用入 ICU 24 小时内的 17 个变量预测延长 ICU 停留（LOS ≥ 72 小时）。随机森林交叉验证 AUROC 为 0.778，测试集 AUROC 为 0.739（95% CI 0.559–0.883），但样本量小、单中心、缺乏外部验证，结果仅用于方法学展示。
